story

Named after the FASTA program (William Pearson, 1985), itself the
successor to FASTP. The format is one line of metadata, then
sequence. That's it. It refuses to die because that's the entire
point.

quirks

Header line conventions are anarchic. ">chr1" vs ">1" vs
">NC_000001.11" vs ">gi|...|ref|NC_000001.11|". Every tool parses
differently. Line-wrap to 60 or 80 chars is conventional but not
required, so naïve byte offsets break.

story

Born at the Sanger Institute as an undocumented internal format,
then retrofitted with a spec after the world had already adopted
three mutually incompatible quality encodings (Sanger, Solexa,
Illumina 1.3+, Illumina 1.5+). The 2009 NAR paper is essentially
archaeology.

quirks

Phred+33 vs Phred+64 quality offsets caused years of silent
miscalls. The 4-line-per-record assumption breaks when sequence
or quality contain '@' or '+'. There is no record terminator.

story

Born on punch cards in 1971 at Brookhaven. The 80-character
record is the IBM 029 keypunch's revenge. The mmCIF transition
took 20+ years and is still incomplete in client tooling.

quirks

Atom serial numbers cap at 99,999. Chain IDs are a single char.
Both got blown up by ribosome and capsid structures. Columns
31–38 are X, 39–46 Y, 47–54 Z. Parse by character position or
you will mis-shift hydrogens.

Multi-file bundles are a long-running scientific anti-pattern: rsync
drops files, tar bundles desync, half a dataset is a non-dataset.
Container formats (GeoPackage, NWB, OME-Zarr-as-directory) are the
modern fix.

hdf5 as universal basement

  • NetCDF-4 (climate)
  • NWB (neuroscience)
  • NeXus (materials)
  • AnnData / Loom (single-cell)
  • HAL (comparative genomics)
  • MINC2 (neuroimaging)
  • BIOM v2 (microbiome)

Whenever a community needed "container + chunks + metadata" between
2005 and 2020, they picked HDF5 and built a schema on top. Zarr is
now repeating the pattern for cloud workloads (OME-Zarr, Zarr-backed
AnnData, NWB-Zarr, the next NetCDF...).

reference implementations that became the spec

  • SAM/BAM (samtools)
  • BED / bigWig (UCSC kent source)
  • VCF (vcftools, then bcftools)
  • NumPy .npy (numpy)
  • Pickle (CPython)

In several cases, the spec is just "whatever this C program does".
Bioinformatics is especially guilty. When a divergent implementation
finds an edge case, the canonical fix is to patch the reference
tool, not the spec.

the xml decade

  • mzML / mzIdentML / mzTab (proteomics)
  • PhyloXML / NeXML (phylogenetics)
  • SBML / BioPAX / SBGN-ML (systems bio)
  • VOTable (astronomy)
  • HL7 CDA
  • GeoJSON's precursor GML
  • KML

~2003–2012 the working assumption was "XML is forever". For
base64-encoded float arrays inside XML this was a disaster of
bytes, and the field has been quietly rotating to HDF5/JSON
ever since.
